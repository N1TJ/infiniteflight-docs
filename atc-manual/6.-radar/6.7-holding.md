# 6.7 Holding



## What is a Hold?

### 6.7.1    

The use of holds can be a very useful tool for Radar Controllers. In particular, they can help with:

 

 -    Managing congested airspace

 -    Aircraft that are high to lose altitude

 -    Sequencing arrivals prior to entering the destination airport environment 

 -    High volume event traffic

 

### 6.7.2 

Controllers operating a radar frequency can assign a holding instruction to an aircraft at fixes and NAVAIDs, such as a VOR, along an aircraft's filed route, to include on an instrument procedure, such as a SID, STAR, or instrument approach. Additionally, controllers may instruct an aircraft to execute a holding pattern at their present position or make a left/right 360º turn. 

### 6.7.3 

To initiate a hold, utilize the drag and vector tool to hover over a fix on the aircraft's flight plan, select hold in the quick menu, choose the direction of turns, and assign an altitude. A speed assignment will automatically be issued based on the altitude assignment given. Aircraft will enter a hold over the assigned fix from their inbound track to that fix. 

Aircraft in a holding pattern can be identified by the hold icon next to the aircraft data tag or by selecting an aircraft on the map to observe the assigned hold. 

To discontinue a hold, tap the aircraft in holding and select "exit hold". Once instructed to exit a hold, aircraft will continue on their flight plan on the next inbound leg. 

 

### 6.7.4

Controllers must ensure a hold, rather than delay vectors or other spacing techniques, will be operationally advanatgeous prior to issuing, as holds can significantly increase aircraft flight time and requires increased vigilance by controllers due to the complexity introduced when deconflicting altitudes and/or overlapping holding patterns.
